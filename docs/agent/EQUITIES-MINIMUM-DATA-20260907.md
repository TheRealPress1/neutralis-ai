## What primary documentation resolves

### SEC evidence and timing

The [SEC API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) documents unauthenticated submissions and XBRL APIs. Submissions update as filings disseminate, with typical subsecond processing; XBRL typically follows within a minute, sometimes longer. Aggregate XBRL covers standard taxonomy/entity-wide facts, while frames select last-filed observations fitting a period. Consequently original accession-specific documents should anchor the memo; today's frame response is not a reconstruction of then-known fundamentals. Custom tags and segment contexts require original filing interpretation. Fiscal period end, filing date, acceptance, dissemination and retrieval are distinct clocks.

[Accessing EDGAR Data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data), last reviewed June 26, 2024, documents free access, an honest declared User-Agent and a maximum 10 requests/second. Original complete submissions, document indexes and headers have documented accession paths. Some evening submissions disseminate on the next business day. Later corrections/removals affect indexes; weekly index rebuilding does not reproduce every historical availability state. CIK is stable for a filer, whereas current ticker associations have no accuracy/scope guarantee. This supports a small prospective capture process with explicit source and receipt times; it does not establish a historical share-class master or an exhaustive corporate-action feed. No actual filing receipt was captured in this task.

The [SEC dissemination policy](https://www.sec.gov/about/privacy-information), last reviewed November 29, 2023, expressly permits copying/further distribution of information on sec.gov without SEC permission and requests attribution; logos/artwork and marks are separately restricted. This is affirmative support for storing and sharing attributed SEC-hosted factual research, rather than an inference from silence. The reviewed text does not separately classify hosted-model inference, model training or third-party intellectual property embedded in exhibits. A narrowly attributed factual packet is the proposed model input; do not represent all linked websites or all embedded materials as licensed by the SEC. No general model-training archive is proposed.

### Daily observations and their meaning

[Alpaca subscription documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api) lists Basic at no charge, US stocks/ETFs, history since 2016 and 200 historical requests/minute. Basic's real-time coverage is IEX; the paid plan adds broader real-time coverage. This is documented product availability, not a verified entitlement for a Neutralis identity, specific instrument or additional endpoint.

The [market-data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq) explicitly permits historical SIP queries without a subscription when the end time is at least 15 minutes old. Specify the feed rather than relying on defaults. It also defines daily bars using New York day boundaries and trade-condition update rules: extended-hours conditions can affect volume without updating daily prices; official-open/close condition messages do not update aggregate OHLC. Therefore a daily aggregate open is not automatically the required official opening price, and daily volume is not automatically regular-session-only volume. Bar timestamps identify interval starts, not the time that a particular corrected response became available. The FAQ documents delayed symbol-rename mapping, another reason to pin identity separately. These distinctions must survive normalization; no IEX/SIP substitution or invented publication timestamp is acceptable.

[Historical bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars) documents explicit raw, split, dividend and spin-off adjustments, with split adjustment changing prices and volume. Its `asof` parameter maps the queried symbol to an entity/name history; it is not a historical data-vintage query. Results are paginated across symbols, with a 10,000-point page maximum. Proposed storage is raw OHLCV plus separately admitted actions. A split-adjusted signal can then use only actions eligible at its decision deadline. Dividend-adjusted prices cannot replace the cash/receivable ledger or be combined with credited dividends without addressing double counting. Current reconstructed adjustment history is not prospective point-in-time evidence.

### Actions, revisions and official-open candidate

[Corporate actions](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/corporateactions-1) lists splits, dividends, spin-offs, cash/stock/mixed mergers, redemptions, name changes, worthless removals, rights, reorganizations, partial calls and capital-gains distributions. Queries use `process_date` boundaries. The `complete` quality filter can exclude unprocessed incomplete records, while processed records can remain incomplete. Crucially, Alpaca disclaims guaranteed action creation timing and warns of upstream/processing delays. Neither that filter nor an empty fully paginated response proves that all ex-date actions through a fill session have arrived. Types being listed also does not establish complete delisted-security coverage, terminal consideration or timely ETF distributions. The [August 27, 2026 changelog](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/changelog/2026-08-27-capital-gains-distributions-fce488a) confirms recently added capital-gains distribution coverage; it does not certify historical completeness.

New useful schema evidence: the [official alpacahq CLI market-data OpenAPI document](https://github.com/alpacahq/cli/blob/main/api/specs/market-data-api.json) defines cash-dividend rate, ex/record/payable/process dates, currency and identifiers; payable date and currency are not universally required. Split schemas expose old/new ratios. Auction records expose price, timestamp, exchange and condition within opening/closing arrays. Corporate-action event envelopes carry mutation, stable action identity, event identity and `at`, explicitly the streaming-service emission time. That is a candidate action source-record clock, distinct from economic process/ex dates. It does not supply a bar revision timestamp. Optional fields and examples establish schema possibilities, not delivered coverage. This live main-branch documentation was viewed, not downloaded or executed; a future implementation must pin the admitted schema version.

The [corporate-action SSE documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/subscribetocorporateactionseventssse) describes insert/update/delete events with historical replay and bounded `since`/`until` intervals. A finite replay audit may therefore be possible without a continuously running listener. Actual access, replay history start, retention, gap handling and whether deleted events remain recoverable are unverified. Receiving all *published mutations* still cannot prove all issuer events were published on time. This new evidence narrows the earlier register's action-vintage gap but does not close it.

[Historical auctions](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockauctionsingle-1) documents date-range auction observations. With the schema above, there is a concrete candidate for official-open validation. Required proof is the exact condition/venue mapping to the instrument's primary regular-session official opening price, handling duplicate/corrected announcements and absence. Selecting the first record, any venue's auction, a 04:00 auction or daily OHLC open would not satisfy v4 by assumption. No documented mapping was fully established here.

The [stock-stream documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/real-time-stock-pricing-data) describes trading-status/halts, trade corrections and updated bars, with access depending on subscription. It does not establish that the proposed finite daily REST package includes complete historical halt intervals or all price revisions. A missing bar is not a positive no-halt observation. A streaming feature is not a reason to start a service under this research authorization.

### Calendar and identity references

The [Alpaca US calendar](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/legacycalendar) documents 1970–2029 trading days and open/close times including early closures. The newer [market calendar](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/calendar-2) adds market identifiers and timezone selection; its prose has an inconsistent XNAS region label, so an identifier must be verified rather than guessed. Neither page establishes complete historical unscheduled-closure/halt provenance. Both describe authenticated routes; none was called.

[NYSE hours/calendars](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars) lists 2026–2028 holidays, Eastern-time schedules and early closes. NYSE Arca has both an early opening auction and a 09:30 core opening auction. Use this as a factual reference check with an explicit America/New_York conversion and a versioned calendar; holidays and per-security halts remain different objects. The [SPY issuer page](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/state-street-spdr-sp-500-etf-trust-spy), dated September 7, 2026 for fund/listing facts, confirms January 22, 1993 inception, NYSE Arca listing, USD, CUSIP 78462F103 and ISIN US78462F1030. This distinguishes instrument inception from a vendor's 2016 history start. Reference facts do not grant blanket rights to issuer-page third-party data or an automated distribution feed.

## Rights remain a separate acceptance decision

The [Alpaca Terms and Conditions](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/TermsAndConditions.pdf), linked from its current [disclosure library](https://alpaca.markets/disclosures), scope ordinary use to personal/noncommercial purposes. Other purposes and applications sharing content trigger advance written notice provisions; copying/transmission for distribution or commercial enterprise requires express prior written consent elsewhere in the same terms. The notice paragraph is not an unconditional permission grant. The terms incorporate other applicable agreements and policies. The reviewed text does not affirm durable two-founder shared research storage, backups after termination, hosted-model processing or organizational backtesting rights. This is an unresolved applicability/entitlement question, not a finding that every personal archive is prohibited. The linked Nasdaq/NYSE subscriber PDFs failed to render in this browser; their exact current provisions were not verified.

Alpaca's [Historical API guide](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/historical-api) expressly describes backtesting as a use case. That supports technical purpose, but does not by itself settle the identity, sharing, retention and processing terms above. Neither a paid quote plan nor a model provider's privacy settings supply missing market-data rights. Conversely the reviewed documentation does not establish that a paid vendor is mandatory. First seek an applicable public agreement or already-held written entitlement for the precise use; root controls any later account, contact or purchase authorization.

Proposed rights receipt must separately mark: private one-user use; two named founder users; organization/project use; durable raw/normalized storage and backups; internal backtests; derived results; external model inference and provider retention; expiry/cancellation deletion; external publication/redistribution. Unknown is a valid distinct status. Until admitted, keep vendor content out of shared model prompts and durable economic archives. Do not relabel a reconstructible price sequence as a harmless derived summary to bypass a restriction. SEC-only factual packets and synthetic cases can be reviewed independently.

The next **separately authorized** proof should be small and finite: one original filing with its header/index linkage and an amendment/timing counterexample; an ordinary stock and SPY bar/auction day; a DST boundary and an early-close day; targeted known examples of dividend, split, rename, terminal cash/stock action and halt/missing observation; and an action update/delete replay. Choose action fixtures by coverage need, not investment merit. Inspect delivered optional fields, all pagination, source clocks, condition/venue mappings and rights before accepting the records. Document exact failing examples rather than hiding gaps behind aggregate success counts. No such sample was fetched here.

For v4 specifically, prove contemporaneous `source_record_time` **and** `retrieved_at` by each decision/fill deadline; positive action coverage; official open and halt status; and the full prescribed warmup/development/validation sessions. Action-event emission times alone do not solve price vintages or calendar vintage evidence. Original receipts must remain immutable; late arrivals/revisions become additional records. If a required source clock, action coverage statement or right cannot be evidenced, report that input unadmitted and leave the corresponding economic observation unexecuted/unresolved. Only co-head acceptance of a revised registration can alter those rules.
