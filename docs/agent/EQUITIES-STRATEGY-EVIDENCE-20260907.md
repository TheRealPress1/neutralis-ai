## What the primary evidence actually supports

**Momentum and profitability.** Jegadeesh and Titman's 2001 follow-up reports that momentum profits continued through the 1990s after the period in their original study, providing some evidence beyond the original sample. The finding motivates relative-strength testing, not an expected return for our long-only implementation. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00342); [author working paper at NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7159.pdf).

Novy-Marx's 2013 paper finds that gross profits divided by assets predict cross-sectional returns and improve value strategies, including among larger, more liquid stocks. Its main profitability-plus-value conclusion does not validate Neutralis's proposed equal-rank momentum-plus-profitability combination. The paper also examines industry-adjusted factor combinations, but those long-short constructions are not our portfolio. Persistent operating quality is an economically interpretable input; sector, valuation and risk explanations still require diagnostics. [Author PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/OSoV.pdf); [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15940).

French's academic momentum construction uses prior months 2–12, a useful reference for the existing skip-month definition. Aggregate factor files are a benchmark, not constituent-level PIT fundamentals, delisting evidence or executable portfolio histories. [French momentum construction](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_mom_factor.html).

Daniel and Moskowitz document severe momentum losses around rebounds following distressed, volatile markets. Their long-short strategy has an especially important losing-stock short leg; its crash magnitude cannot be assigned to a long-only winners portfolio. It nevertheless motivates explicit rebound and concentration diagnostics. [Author paper via NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439.pdf).

**Replication and implementability constrain conviction.** McLean and Pontiff study 97 published predictors and find average portfolio returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. These aggregate estimates are evidence of selection/publication effects, not a mechanical haircut or failure probability for our particular factors. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365).

Hou, Xue and Zhang's published study evaluates 452 anomalies. After limiting microcap influence through NYSE breakpoints and value weighting, 65% fail a conventional significance threshold and 82% fail their higher multiple-testing threshold. These are broad replication findings, not a finding that every momentum or profitability specification fails. They reinforce our large/liquid universe and the need to distinguish equal-weight stock-selection effects from size exposure. [Published paper](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2019/5236964).

Novy-Marx and Velikov find that a spread between entry and retention thresholds is an effective simple cost-mitigation technique. Their results favor lower-turnover anomalies after implementation costs; profitability has more capacity than many faster strategies. This motivates a preregistered buffer comparison, not silently changing the current top-quintile experiment after observing its results. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063); [author PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf).

**Broad-index trend.** Clare, Seaton, Smith and Thomas compare S&P 500 moving-average and other rules. Their longer monthly analysis covers January 1952–June 2011; the shorter daily-return analysis covers July 1988–June 2011. With their assumed 20 basis points for each buy and sell, slower rules and month-end decisions often improve historical risk-adjusted results; extra stop-loss rules do not help in their tests. This is a historical parameter comparison, not a prospectively frozen contemporary ETF experiment. It supports testing a simple rule, without adopting the best historical parameter as a newly discovered optimum. [Published-paper repository record](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17842/); [author manuscript](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17842/8/BLACKBOX%20%20%20SSRN-id2126476.pdf).

Bajgrowicz and Scaillet supply a useful challenge: across a large technical-rule universe on DJIA data from 1897–2011, apparent in-sample winners cannot reliably be selected prospectively, and costs erase much apparent profitability. This does not directly disprove a fixed monthly long-only S&P/cash rule; it warns against searching many lookbacks and reporting only the winner. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.001); [author PDF](https://scaillet.ch/pdfs/BajSca.pdf).

**LLM signals versus a multi-agent architecture.** Lopez-Lira and Tang's October 2025 revision separates immediate news reaction from later tradable drift. Their October 2021–May 2024 overnight-news long-short strategy reports Sharpe 2.97 before costs, 1.29 at 10 basis points round-trip and negative performance at 20 basis points. Daily turnover is roughly 186–190%, depending on the reported calculation; value-weighted results are much weaker. This supports a conditional information-processing hypothesis, not a liquid long-only, low-turnover mandate or a claim about Dr. Lira's live portfolio. [Exact author version examined, v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6).

Anic, Barbon, Seiz and Zarattini provide a closer long-only example: news-based LLM scores modify selection and weights inside an S&P 500 momentum portfolio. They select among 512 configurations using October 2019–December 2023 and test January 2024–March 2025. Reported test Sharpe rises from 0.79 to 1.06 under a 2-basis-point transaction-cost assumption; turnover rises. The 15-month test is short and the reported alpha is significant only at 10%. The proprietary return data and unspecified exact model snapshot limit independent reproduction. Their claim that passing a pretraining cutoff guarantees no leakage is stronger than the evidence warrants. Treat this as a promising single-model overlay hypothesis, not proof for multiple PMs or all-in net returns. [Author preprint, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26228v1).

TradingAgents illustrates specialized analysts, debate, a trader and risk roles, but its reported test covers only January 1–March 29, 2024, with individual stock examples and long/short trades. Its authors note 11 LLM calls and over 20 tool calls per prediction; cost constrained test length. We did not find an explicit transaction-cost or slippage model in the examined text. The demonstration does not isolate coordination value or establish diversified long-only performance. [Author paper, v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v7).

Two important evidence checks: Kim, Muhn and Nikolaev's *Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models* was withdrawn on February 20, 2025 after a co-author found data/analysis inconsistencies during replication. Its old earnings-accuracy and Sharpe claims should not count as positive mandate evidence. [Current author withdrawal notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17866). Levy's 2026 *Journal of Accounting Research* paper finds that a numerical perturbation reduces GPT-4 earnings-direction accuracy from 60% to 51% in the studied setting, and discusses post-training information beyond nominal pretraining cutoffs. This warrants contamination and numerical-robustness checks; it does not establish that every LLM financial task fails. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.70058).

## Concrete research choices, without changing the accepted work

1. **Preserve the factor core.** Test profitability-only, momentum-only, equal-weight eligible universe and their existing combined score on identical coverage. Keep existing development/validation/test boundaries, costs, paired inference and sector/size diagnostics. A possible later buffer variant could enter the top 20% and retain until below a separately fixed threshold; co-heads must freeze that threshold, weighting, rebalance behavior and additional hypothesis count before results. Do not optimize these against the held-out period.
2. **Add a cheap trend comparator only by co-head specification.** One candidate is month-end price above its 200-trading-day average, with the next eligible session's execution, otherwise the explicitly modeled cash vehicle. Freeze price-versus-total-return signal, holidays, equality/missing-data behavior, initial state, fill timing and actual net cash yield. Compare to buy-and-hold of the same investable ETF and a predeclared static equity/cash allocation; this helps separate reduced equity exposure from timing value. Pre-ETF index history may provide research context but cannot be labeled an executed ETF history. Do not mix this rule into the factorial experiment without recording a separate hypothesis.
3. **Make the team prove a narrow increment.** Preserve shared frozen analyst packets for one PM versus two independent PMs, identical hard controls and joint acceptance of the complete discretionary rebalance. This isolates PM-stage coordination, not analyst value. A later, separate analyst-value or LLM-overlay comparison would need the same non-LLM candidate set and portfolio constraints. Use bounded weekly/event decision jobs only if co-heads freeze that cadence; do not assume shorter holding periods produce better catalyst capture. A short forward run can establish logging, decision consistency and cost behavior, but cannot establish durable alpha.

## Data and cost conditions for an honest result

These are implementation requirements and unresolved procurement questions, not assertions that any vendor's license is sufficient.

| Requirement | Factor strategy | Trend/cash comparator | Fundamental/catalyst team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Point in time | Historical eligible securities and identifiers; original filing availability/version; fiscal alignment; corporate actions and delisting proceeds; no current-constituent substitution | As-of signal/fill prices and distributions; true tradable instrument inception; available cash yield and calendar | Publication and first-availability timestamps, original/revised documents, event dates, timestamped consensus if used, duplicate/stale-news treatment; model and prompt version |
| Rights to verify | Permitted historical storage, derived metrics, internal backtests and any later display/redistribution | Price/distribution/cash-series usage, storage and display | Document/news retention plus permission for third-party model processing, provider retention and later display of outputs/excerpts |
| Reproducibility | Source receipts and frozen eligible rows; missing/stale/revised-data diagnostics | One frozen rule and action/price/cash ledger | Immutable source packet and cutoff, numerical outputs from code, cited facts separated from judgments, no browsing during decisions, bounded rounds and recorded failures |

Published papers and publicly downloadable factor series do not demonstrate that Neutralis has the underlying data or usage rights. A dated prompt does not remove information already embedded in a model. The cleanest new model test is a prospective freeze with contemporaneously archived inputs and predictions; historical exercises remain useful but require explicit contamination limits. Changed models, source feeds or prompts should be versioned as experiment changes.

For annual incremental running cost `C` and invested capital `K`, the approximate fixed-cost return drag is `C / K`. For illustration only, $600 per year consumes 12% of $5,000, 2.4% of $25,000, or 0.6% of $100,000. These are arithmetic scenarios, not budgets or quoted bills. The relevant comparison is incremental performance **over the chosen baseline** after trading and incremental running costs, not whether gross returns exceed zero.

Count incremental data, model and hosting charges; report shared platform costs separately. Record uncached input, cached input and output without double-counting cached tokens, and include failed/retried research calls. A monthly deterministic signal has no inherent LLM expense. A team should run bounded jobs on a fixed candidate shortlist, reuse permitted unchanged packets and stop after its accepted round limit. Data/history fees may dominate token costs. Report both trading-cost-net research returns and fully allocated investor economics; preserve the existing 10/25/50-basis-point factor cost scenarios rather than replacing them with the LLM paper's 2-basis-point assumption. Taxes depend on account and turnover and remain a separately stated omission unless explicitly modeled.

**Three decisions for Fable/Astra:** keep the existing factor experiment as the evidence-based signal comparison; consider a fixed trend/cash rule as the cheapest additional comparator; require forward-paper incremental evidence and measured costs before treating a two-PM investment team as an alpha source. These are recommendations for co-head review, not competing dispatch or mandate selection.
