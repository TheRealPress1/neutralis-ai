# First equity experiment: does profitability improve momentum?

Prepared 2026-09-06 by Astra, incorporating Fable's independent supplier review and challenge plus a bounded independent hypothesis review. Status: concrete proposal for discussion, not a frozen implementation ticket. No historical dataset has been purchased or audited, no backtest has been run, and no performance result is claimed.

The question is whether selecting profitable businesses improves a simple momentum portfolio after trading costs, at comparable risk. This directly tests Constantine's idea that a known company metric may help predict subsequent returns. It also creates a baseline against which later agent-generated ideas can be measured.

| Candidate | Research question | First-pass decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price momentum | Do recent relative winners continue to outperform? | Required baseline; simplest data requirement |
| Momentum plus gross profitability | Does company profitability add useful information beyond momentum? | Preferred first substantive experiment, conditional on adequate historical data |
| Post-earnings-announcement drift | Do stocks continue moving after a measurable earnings surprise? | Defer: release timing and the definition/data for the surprise add dependencies |

The French momentum factor uses prior months 2–12 and monthly formation. Novy-Marx documents gross profits/assets, excluding financials, with annual accounting and principally annual portfolio sorts; his central combination is profitability with value. Our monthly, long-only combination is an adaptation requiring its own evidence. [French construction](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_mom_factor.html), [Novy-Marx author manuscript](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/OSoV.pdf)

Earnings surprise measured against analyst forecasts produces a different test from surprise measured using historical earnings; an earnings-growth field is not a substitute for archived analyst expectations. [Livnat–Mendenhall](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2006.00196.x)

## Proposed rules

These are deliberately fixed starting choices, not optimized parameters or trading recommendations. Resolve data feasibility, then freeze this specification before examining strategy returns.

1. **Historical market universe.** Reconstruct US domestic common stocks listed on NYSE, Nasdaq or NYSE American at each month-end. Exclude funds, preferred shares and ADRs. Exclude SIC 6000–6999, including financial firms/REITs, using a dated classification. Select one share class per issuer by trailing liquidity where several qualify. Require an unadjusted close of at least $5, at least 13 month-end price observations and average daily dollar volume of at least $10 million over the preceding 60 trading sessions. Take the largest 500 eligible issuers by then-known market capitalization. Share counts, classifications and membership must have historical evidence; today's ticker snapshot is insufficient. These size/liquidity cutoffs are proposed engineering choices.
2. **Fundamentals subset.** Within that market universe, require positive assets and a usable annual gross-profit measure from an original filing available before the decision. Report monthly exclusions by count, capitalization and reason. Apply the same subset to all four core portfolios below. Separately run the momentum and equal-weight reference on the broader market universe to measure the effect of missing fundamentals. Do not require future coverage or survival to enter either universe.
3. **Momentum.** Rank total returns over the preceding twelve months excluding the latest month. At month-end `m`, use `TRI(m-1) / TRI(m-12) - 1`, where TRI is a validated total-return index. For example, a September-end signal measures the return from the previous September-end through August-end. This is eleven monthly returns, with September skipped.
4. **Profitability.** Use annual `(revenue - cost of revenue/goods sold) / total assets`, with compatible units and the same fiscal-year vintage. Prefer original reported gross profit where its definition is reconciled. Use the most recent annual period whose original filing was available, and exclude observations with fiscal year-end more than 18 months old. Missing costs are not zero; negative gross profit is allowed. Freeze the field mapping before return analysis. A later restatement must never overwrite the earlier decision's inputs.
5. **Comparisons.** Each month form an equal-weight eligible-subset reference, top-quintile momentum portfolio, top-quintile profitability portfolio and top-quintile combined portfolio. Combined score is the equal average of the two ascending percentile ranks. Select `ceil(N/5)` names; break ties by stable security ID. Target weights are equal among selected names. Maintain the same accounting and execution assumptions across portfolios. No shorts or leverage. These are research portfolios; a practical position count/capital amount would need a later specification.
6. **Availability and execution.** Freeze features at 23:59 US Eastern on the last trading day of the month and simulate execution at the next trading session's open. Inputs need both public availability evidence and a documented feasible delivery delay. When only a filing date is known, impose one complete trading-session lag; if the required feed would not yet have delivered a value, defer it. Use unadjusted executable prices, reconciling any reconstruction from split-adjusted OHLC. Trade sizes must use pretrade information and enforce available cash; do not size orders from an opening price learned only at execution. Missing tradable prices do not create fills. Freeze cash-reserve, rounding and suspended-security handling with the ledger contract before running returns.
7. **Returns and costs.** Audit splits, dividends, spinoffs, merger consideration and final delisting proceeds without double counting. Use an explicit cash/position ledger. Test 10, 25 and 50 basis points per side on dollars actually traded, including entry and exit. At 25 bp, buying and later selling $10,000 costs about $50 before price changes. These are sensitivity assumptions, not observed execution estimates. Measure turnover, break-even costs and order size relative to lagged volume; actual capacity, broker fees and slippage require later calibration. Report pre-tax results; taxes and continuing data/compute expenses must be included in a later capital-specific economic assessment.

The detailed data conditions and sample audit are in [DATA-SHORTLIST.md](DATA-SHORTLIST.md). Proposed cash, sizing, dividend, action and reconstruction conventions are now specified in [RESEARCH-DATA-CONTRACT.md](RESEARCH-DATA-CONTRACT.md); freeze the documents together after sample review. In particular, delisting from an exchange is not automatically a total loss. An assumed terminal haircut can be reported as a sensitivity bound, but cannot be labeled verified history. If unresolved outcomes materially affect the result, the experiment remains invalid or inconclusive.

## Evaluation and advancement

Proposed windows, conditional on audited coverage: development 2000–2012, validation 2013–2019, final historical test 2020–2025; preceding history is for signal initialization. Reserve 2026 as an additional locked follow-up period. If usable coverage is shorter, declare new windows before examining returns and record the resulting loss of evidence. Do not silently substitute a five-year dataset for this design.

The primary outcome is the average monthly **net return difference between combination and momentum on the identical fundamentals subset**, with a dependence-aware confidence interval. Proposed inference: a paired circular block bootstrap of monthly differences, 12-month blocks, 10,000 resamples and fixed seed 20260906, with preregistered 6- and 24-month block sensitivity. Report a two-sided 95% interval, all three cost cases and the limited power of a short final window; do not pick a block length or cost case because it produces significance. Report compounded wealth, annualized returns, volatility, drawdown/recovery, turnover, concentration and sector/size exposures. Include the profitability-only and broader-universe results so we can distinguish a useful combination from a standalone factor or coverage effect.

Preregister one sector-controlled diagnostic: compare selection within the same historically classified sectors while matching the reference sector weights. Fix the sector mapping and sparse-sector handling before results. Also report whether market and size exposures explain the incremental return. These diagnostics assess attribution, rather than giving us new portfolios from which to select a winner. Report development, validation and final periods separately, including major market-rebound episodes. Bootstrap separately within each evaluation period; never pool development observations into the final-test interval.

Retain every variant and correction in an experiment registry. The four core portfolios and two broader-universe controls are comparisons, not six chances to declare the best observed one successful. Any subsequent parameter or hypothesis change becomes a new registered experiment; repeatedly inspecting the final period spends its independence.

| Finding | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Missing availability evidence, unreliable identity joins or unresolved material corporate actions | Test invalid; repair the data before interpreting returns |
| Final-period net combination-minus-momentum mean is nonpositive | Fail the stated economic hypothesis |
| Positive incremental mean but confidence interval includes zero | Inconclusive; do not claim an edge |
| Apparent benefit disappears under planned sector/size diagnostics or plausible costs | Do not advance the claimed stock-selection effect |
| Positive, independently reproduced evidence that survives the planned checks | Candidate for a separately specified forward paper experiment, not real-money approval |

Published results often weaken on independent examination. McLean–Pontiff document out-of-sample and post-publication decay across 97 predictors; Hou–Xue–Zhang show how microcap treatment and statistical standards change replication results. Neither provides a predicted return or success probability for this proposal. [McLean–Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12365), [Hou–Xue–Zhang](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2019/5236964)

Momentum also warrants specific rebound diagnostics. The classic crash evidence studies long-short portfolios, including sharp rebounds in past losers; those crash magnitudes cannot be transferred directly to our long-only portfolio. [Daniel–Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)

Historical holdouts are unexamined by this experiment, not unknown to the literature or today's models. The first experiment uses explicit numeric rules; Astra and Fable help research, implement and challenge them. Later forward paper decisions must be recorded before outcomes arrive, with the actual data, code and any model outputs frozen. A few successful monthly decisions are insufficient validation.
